   1. Fuselage Tray Alignment: Locate Avionics Bay 3A on the forward fuselage port flank. Verify that the racking tray is completely free of dirt, sea spray salt residue, or debris.
   2. Track Engagement: Lift the Univac IX chassis using the front-panel stainless steel quick-extraction handles. Align the left and right continuous outer shelf flanges (f14_bay_mounting_flanges) with the NATO-standard tracking shelves in Bay 3A.
   3. Insertion & Mating: Smoothly push the chassis backward along the tracks. As the box reaches maximum depth, the rear-facing ARINC 404A beveled guide pins will automatically find the receiver sockets. Continue pushing until the floating data contacts and the self-sealing concentric hydraulic cooling stabs are fully seated.
   4. Primary Structural Hold-Down: Push inward on the lower-left and lower-right captive front-panel thumbscrews (arinc_chassis_holddown_screws). Rotate the oversized wing-nut heads clockwise to engage the aircraft racking hooks. Torque both screws to 28-32 in-lbs.
   5. Chassis Frame Grounding: Route the pre-attached braided copper grounding strap out from the rear corner of the box. Secure the terminal lug clamp tightly to the nearest structural fuselage airframe ground post using a calibrated wrench set to 15 in-lbs to prevent marine EMI.
   6. Internal Card Servicing (If Applicable): To slide out an individual module, push the red locking release button on the front panel handle, extract the module down its inner slider rails, and replace. When locking cards inside, torque the internal hex-cap retention screws to 8.5 in-lbs to secure them against carrier landing impacts.

   1. Intake De-Ice Check: Ensure the F-14 engine intake variable ramps are driven to the fully open position. Wipe down the interior intake trunk walls with isopropyl alcohol to remove acoustic oil film.
   2. Segment Insertion: Take the multi-piece RAM-impregnated composite S-duct sleeve (engine_intake_s_duct). Compress the internal split seam slightly to narrow its profile and pass it through the front intake lips.
   3. Seating Matrix: Push the sleeve down the trunk until it bottoms out against the mechanical stop barrier forward of the engine fan face. Release the split compression; the composite structure will snap outward, locking itself tight against the interior skin walls via friction-fit mechanical tension.
   4. Flow-Vane Alignment: Visually inspect through the inlet. Verify that the internal vertical silver guide vanes are straight. This confirms that incoming air will snake smoothly through the double-curved S-path, keeping engine operation fully stable while completely hiding the spinning fan blades from forward enemy radar.

## Part B: Supersonic Tail Fin Plasma Actuator Clamp

   1. Actuator Layer Alignment: Lay the flexible Kapton-dielectric cold plasma ribbon actuator (embedded_actuator_ribbon) flat inside the internal relief channel of the Ti-6Al-4V titanium leading edge glove (supersonic_retention_clamp).
   2. Glove Engagement: Position the sleeve assembly over the front apex nose of the vertical stabilizer fin. Push the wrap-around clamp rearward until it completely blankets the apex. The aerodynamic curve must hug the tail fin smoothly.
   3. Tension Wedge Cinching: Locate the three trailing-edge tension wedge blocks on the rear split geometry of the clamp. Insert the three high-tensile socket-head tension bolts.
   4. Torque Validation: Using a cross-pattern sequence, torque the three rear hex bolts to 45 in-lbs. This squeezes the titanium sleeve firmly around the tail fin's natural aerodynamic taper. The structural friction fit keeps the assembly from moving under supersonic Mach 2.34 friction, while the recessed relief channel shields the delicate plasma electrodes from high-speed boundary layer airflow shear stress.
   5. Power Feed Hookup: Route the waterproof shielded conduit track (complete_wing_bridge) from the avionics bay connection out across the wing-glove cavity. Connect the high-voltage lines directly to the actuator input terminals. Ensure the interlocking polymer segments pivot freely up to 60 degrees to prevent any binding or pinching during variable wing-sweep cycles.
